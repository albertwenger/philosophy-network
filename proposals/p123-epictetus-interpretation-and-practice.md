# Interpreting philosophy should lead into practice

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Understanding a philosophical text matters through the use one makes of its teaching.

## Context and limits

The passage criticizes displaying interpretive skill while failing to live accordingly. It treats textual understanding as necessary preparation rather than sufficient philosophical achievement.

## Source excerpt

> And when I find an Interpreter, what remains is, to make use of his Instructions. This alone is the valuable Thing. But, if I admire nothing but merely the Interpretation, what do I become more than a Grammarian, instead of a Philosopher? Except, indeed, that, instead of Homer, I interpret Chrysippus. When any one therefore desires me to read Chrysippus to him, I rather blush, when I cannot show my Actions agreeable, and consonant to his Discourse.

Elizabeth Carter translation (1759). Section XLIX, concluding paragraph on the interpreter and the use of instructions.
