# Rights and duties stand together

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Denying women the rights of rational agents undermines the demand that they fulfil moral duties.

## Context and limits

The closing argument insists that women share rights with men and should be free in physical, moral, and civil life. Its discussion of a husband’s absolute rule is an ironic consequence drawn from denying women reason, not an endorsement of that rule. Shared rights accompany accountability rather than exemption from criticism.

## Source excerpt

> Asserting the rights which women in common with men ought to contend for, I have not attempted to extenuate their faults; but to prove them to be the natural consequence of their education and station in society. If so, it is reasonable to suppose, that they will change their character, and correct their vices and follies, when they are allowed to be free in a physical, moral, and civil sense.

English text, Chapter XIII, concluding discussion; the subsequent paragraphs connect rights with duties.
