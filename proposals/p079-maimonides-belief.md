# Belief requires understanding

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Saying a doctrine is not yet understanding and believing it.

## Context and limits

In his discussion of divine unity, Maimonides distinguishes verbal profession from conviction about reality. He requires true conviction to survive rational scrutiny. This is an intellectual account of belief within a theological argument, not a description of every use of faith.

## Source excerpt

> For belief is only possible after the apprehension of a thing

M. Friedländer translation; I.L, p. 68.

## Source and attribution

[Maimonides](../people/maimonides.md); [Guide for the Perplexed](../sources/maimonides-guide.md).

Passage: Part I, chapter L, especially the opening and the definition of belief on pp. 67–68.
