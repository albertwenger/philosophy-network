# Why universal indifference cannot be willed

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

We cannot coherently will a universal refusal of aid while willing help in our own possible need.

## Context and limits

Unlike deceptive promising, universal non-assistance could exist. [Kant](../people/kant.md) locates the contradiction in willing such a world while needing others’ help. The example supports an imperfect duty to promote others’ welfare; it does not specify a fixed act, recipient, or amount of aid, and it is not a bargain for repayment.

## Source excerpt

> But although it is possible that a universal law of nature might exist in accordance with that maxim, it is impossible to will that such a principle should have the universal validity of a law of nature. For a will which resolved this would contradict itself, inasmuch as many cases might occur in which one would have need of the love and sympathy of others, and in which, by such a law of nature, sprung from his own will, he would deprive himself of all hope of the aid he desires.

Thomas Kingsmill Abbott translation, Section II, Academy 4:423; “that maxim” refers to refusing assistance.
