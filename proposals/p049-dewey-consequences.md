# Learning from what an action brings about

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Experience becomes instructive when we connect what we do with the consequences we undergo.

## Context and limits

Neither activity alone nor a succession of sensations guarantees learning. Perceiving the connection gives a past event meaning for future action. [Dewey](../people/dewey.md) uses a child’s encounter with a flame to distinguish merely being changed from understanding the relation that produced the change; the example describes learning rather than recommending exposure to danger.

## Source excerpt

> To "learn from experience" is to make a backward and forward connection between what we do to things and what we enjoy or suffer from things in consequence. Under such conditions, doing becomes a trying; an experiment with the world to find out what it is like; the undergoing becomes instruction—discovery of the connection of things.

English text, Chapter XI, section 1.
