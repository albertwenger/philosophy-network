# Pain and the union of mind and body

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Pain and hunger express a lived union of mind and body.

## Context and limits

The meditator contrasts feeling bodily injury with merely observing damage to a vessel. These sensations reveal a union, even though the meditation also argues that mind and body are distinct. Union here does not erase that distinction.

## Source excerpt

> I am very closely united to it

Haldane and Ross translation; Meditation VI, p. 192, referring to the body.

[René Descartes](../people/descartes.md); [Meditations on First Philosophy](../sources/descartes-meditations.md).
