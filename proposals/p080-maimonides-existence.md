# Existence and divine essence

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

For God alone, uncaused existence is not something added to an essence.

## Context and limits

Maimonides distinguishes caused beings from a being whose existence depends on no cause. He rejects treating God as a substance bearing an additional attribute of existence. The argument presupposes his account of divine simplicity; it is not a neutral definition of every existent.

## Source excerpt

> existence and essence are perfectly identical

M. Friedländer translation; I.LVII, p. 80, concerning God.
