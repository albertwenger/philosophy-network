# Equity corrects an overgeneral rule

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

A just judgment may correct a general law where an exceptional case escapes its wording.

## Context and limits

Equity is a correction of legal justice, not permission to discard justice. General laws must speak about usual cases, while practical affairs vary. The correction asks what the legislator would have said if present and aware of the case; it therefore needs judgment about particulars.

## Source excerpt

> When the law speaks universally, then, and a case arises on it which is not covered by the universal statement, then it is right, where the legislator fails us and has erred by oversimplicity, to correct the omission-to say what the legislator himself would have said had he been present, and would have put into his law if he had known.

W. D. Ross translation, V.10, 1137b19–24; hyphenation follows the archive.
