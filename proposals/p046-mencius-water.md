# Human nature and the direction of water

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Human nature tends toward goodness, although external force can divert that tendency.

## Context and limits

Gaozi compares human nature to water that can flow east or west. [Mencius](../people/mencius.md) answers by distinguishing those directions from water’s tendency downward. His analogy defends a direction of development; it does not deny wrongdoing or establish that goodness develops without supportive circumstances.

## Source excerpt

> Mencius replied, 'Water indeed will flow indifferently to the east or west, but will it flow indifferently up or down? The tendency of man's nature to good is like the tendency of water to flow downwards. There are none but have this tendency to good, just as all water flows downwards.

James Legge translation, 6A2; the reply continues with water forced upward.

[Mencius](../people/mencius.md); [The Works of Mencius](../sources/mencius-works.md).
