# Knowledge requires reflection on sensation

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Sensation alone does not supply the reflection through which knowledge reaches truth.

## Context and limits

Socrates distinguishes seeing and hearing from comparing their objects as alike, different, or existing. Theaetetus accepts that this comparison belongs to the soul’s own activity. The passage rejects knowledge as mere perception; it does not yet complete the dialogue’s search for a definition of knowledge.

## Source excerpt

> knowledge does not consist in impressions of sense, but in reasoning about them

Benjamin Jowett translation; Socrates speaking, 186d.
