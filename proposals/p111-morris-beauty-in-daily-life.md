# Beauty belongs in ordinary life

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Everyday surroundings can be shaped for use and beauty without becoming displays of wealth.

## Context and limits

Morris permits costly decoration when it is enjoyed for beauty rather than show. His household rule belongs to a wider effort to restore art to ordinary living; simplicity is not a ban on artistic richness.

## Source excerpt

> This simplicity you may make as costly as you please or can, on the other hand: you may hang your walls with tapestry instead of whitewash or paper; or you may cover them with mosaic, or have them frescoed by a great painter: all this is not luxury, if it be done for beauty’s sake, and not for show: it does not break our golden rule: *Have nothing in your houses which you do not know to be useful or believe to be beautiful*.

The Beauty of Life, household rule and paragraph distinguishing beauty from show.
