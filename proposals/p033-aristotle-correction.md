# Correcting injury between equals

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

In correcting injury, a judge treats the parties as equals and addresses the unequal loss and gain.

## Context and limits

Unlike distribution according to merit, this correction does not turn on whether the injured person or wrongdoer is morally better. [Aristotle](../people/aristotle.md) applies the account to voluntary and involuntary transactions. He acknowledges that the language of gain and loss fits some injuries imperfectly; the account is not a literal claim that every wound enriches its author.

## Source excerpt

> For it makes no difference whether a good man has defrauded a bad man or a bad man a good one, nor whether it is a good or a bad man that has committed adultery; the law looks only to the distinctive character of the injury, and treats the parties as equal, if one is in the wrong and the other is being wronged, and if one inflicted injury and the other has received it.

W. D. Ross translation, V.4, 1132a2–6.
