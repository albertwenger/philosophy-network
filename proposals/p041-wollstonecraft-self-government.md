# Power to govern oneself

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Education should enable women to govern themselves through reason.

## Context and limits

[Wollstonecraft](../people/wollstonecraft.md) replies to Rousseau’s claim that educating women like men would diminish their influence over men. Her goal is independent judgment and the ability to discharge life’s duties. The passage contrasts self-government with influence based on cultivated dependence and attractiveness; it does not reject affection.

## Source excerpt

> It is true, they could not then with equal propriety be termed the sweet flowers that smile in the walk of man; but they would be more respectable members of society, and discharge the important duties of life by the light of their own reason. "Educate women like men," says Rousseau, "and the more they resemble our sex the less power will they have over us." This is the very point I aim at. I do not wish them to have power over men; but over themselves.

English text, Chapter IV; Rousseau’s words are quoted within Wollstonecraft’s argument.
