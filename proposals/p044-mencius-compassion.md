# Compassion as a moral beginning

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Spontaneous concern for a child in danger reveals a beginning of benevolence that needs development.

## Context and limits

[Mencius](../people/mencius.md) asks us to consider a sudden response before calculations about praise or advantage. Compassion is one of four moral beginnings, not the whole of virtue. The thought experiment supports his claim about shared human capacities; the passage itself still distinguishes possessing a beginning from bringing it to maturity.

## Source excerpt

> When I say that all men have a mind which cannot bear to see the sufferings of others, my meaning may be illustrated thus: even now-a-days, if men suddenly see a child about to fall into a well, they will without exception experience a feeling of alarm and distress.
>
> […]
>
> The feeling of commiseration is the principle of benevolence.

James Legge translation, 2A6. The intervening rejection of ulterior motives and the other moral feelings are omitted; “principle” renders a beginning.
