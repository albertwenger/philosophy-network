# Dignity admits no equivalent

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Rational nature capable of morality has dignity: a worth for which no equivalent can be substituted.

## Context and limits

The contrast is with market and taste values. [Kant](../people/kant.md) grounds dignity in the capacity for moral legislation, not in usefulness, social rank, or successful outcomes. Humanity is used here for rational nature; the passage does not provide an empirical test for deciding which particular humans deserve respect.

## Source excerpt

> In the kingdom of ends everything has either value or dignity. Whatever has a value can be replaced by something else which is equivalent; whatever, on the other hand, is above all value, and therefore admits of no equivalent, has a dignity.
>
> […]
>
> Thus morality, and humanity as capable of it, is that which alone has dignity.

Thomas Kingsmill Abbott translation, Section II, Academy 4:434–435. The intervening distinction between market, fancy, and intrinsic worth is omitted.
