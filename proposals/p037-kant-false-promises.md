# The self-defeat of false promising

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

A rule allowing deceptive promises in need undermines the practice it depends on when universalized.

## Context and limits

The example concerns someone seeking a loan while knowing repayment is impossible. [Kant](../people/kant.md) argues that universal permission to promise without intending performance would destroy credibility. This tests the maxim’s universal form, not merely the likely inconvenience of this one deception. Whether particular formulations genuinely contradict themselves remains an interpretive question.

## Source excerpt

> For supposing it to be a universal law that everyone when he thinks himself in a difficulty should be able to promise whatever he pleases, with the purpose of not keeping his promise, the promise itself would become impossible, as well as the end that one might have in view in it, since no one would consider that anything was promised to him, but would ridicule all such statements as vain pretences.

Thomas Kingsmill Abbott translation, Section II, Academy 4:422.
