# Make room before assenting to an insult

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

A pause can help us examine the opinion through which we feel provoked.

## Context and limits

The passage directs attention to judging an appearance before being carried away by it. Its claim about feeling affronted does not establish that a physical blow causes no bodily injury.

## Source excerpt

> Remember, that not he who gives Ill Language, or a Blow, affronts; but the Principle, which represents these Things as affronting. When therefore, any one provokes you, be assured, that it is your own Opinion which provokes you. Try, therefore, in the first place, not to be hurried away with the Appearance. For, if you once gain Time and Respite, you will more easily command yourself.

Elizabeth Carter translation (1759). Section XX in full.
