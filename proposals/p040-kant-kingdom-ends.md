# A community of ends and lawmakers

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Regard yourself and others as members of an ideal community of ends under common moral laws.

## Context and limits

Members give universal laws while remaining subject to them. The kingdom is a moral ideal, not an existing state or a license for anyone to impose private preferences. Its members’ dignity rests on rational moral legislation; membership requires respecting other rational beings as ends.

## Source excerpt

> A rational being belongs as a member to the kingdom of ends when, although giving universal laws in it, he is also himself subject to these laws.
>
> […]
>
> It follows incontestably that, to whatever laws any rational being may be subject, he being an end in himself must be able to regard himself as also legislating universally in respect of these same laws, since it is just this fitness of his maxims for universal legislation that distinguishes him as an end in himself; also it follows that this implies his dignity (prerogative) above all mere physical beings, that he must always take his maxims from the point of view which regards himself and, likewise, every other rational being as law-giving beings (on which account they are called persons).

Thomas Kingsmill Abbott translation, Section II, Academy 4:433 and 4:438. Intervening argument omitted.
