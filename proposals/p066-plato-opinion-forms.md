# Opinion and what its objects are

The short version and commentary are draft interpretations, not quotations.

## Short version

Recognizing beautiful things differs from understanding the beauty in which they participate.

## Context and limits

Socrates distinguishes the lover of sights from someone who recognizes beauty itself without confusing it with particular beautiful things. In this argument opinion concerns the many appearances, while knowledge concerns what they participate in. The distinction belongs to the dialogue’s account of Forms, not a claim that every everyday opinion is false.

## Source excerpt

> the mind of the other, who opines only, has opinion

Benjamin Jowett translation; Socrates speaking, 476d.
