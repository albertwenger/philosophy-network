# Construction findings

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: draft editorial assessment; no reader results.

## Assessment

This pilot organizes 30 philosophical passages into short interpretations
linked to sources and distinct concept senses. It shows how the proposed
structure can be populated and identifies places where shortening may change
meaning. Whether readers understand these versions accurately, and with less
effort, remains untested.

The work provides materials for independent source review and a reader study.
It does not establish that the sense distinctions are adequate or that the
sample represents philosophy as a whole.

## Work completed

The pilot contains 30 proposals and study cases, 25 concept pages, 13
contributor pages, and 19 source records. The selected passages cover nine
overlapping forms of writing. One argument and two open questions illustrate
how links can identify an inference or a point needing explanation.

Each proposal has a short version, context and limits, a source reference,
and links to concept senses. Each study case has a reader question, a draft
answer guide, and an expectation about the context needed. The assistant
prepared both the interpretations and their assessments. Neither independent
reviewers nor human participants have evaluated them.

## Observations from construction

1. **Related senses can be kept distinct.** The [self](../concepts/self.md)
   and [freedom](../concepts/freedom.md) pages group interpretations while
   preserving differences among them. The proposed boundaries need review.
2. **Small words can carry decisive content.** The short version in
   [Case 16](cases/c016-wittgenstein-meaning.md) retains a restriction on scope.
   [Case 11](cases/c011-kant-humanity.md) proposes a question about the
   distinction carried by *merely*. Removing either restriction changes
   the claim.
3. **A link can move a difficulty without explaining it.**
   [Case 1](cases/c001-aristotle-mean.md) identifies reason and practical wisdom
   as the standard for the mean. Its [open question](../questions/q001-appropriateness.md)
   asks what readers need to apply that standard. Naming it does not settle
   how to use it.
4. **An adaptation needs its own attribution.** Cases
   [14](cases/c014-mill-harm.md), [19](cases/c019-epictetus-judgment.md), and
   [27](cases/c027-wollstonecraft-virtue.md) distinguish interpretations of a
   source from broader or weaker adaptations. Each substantive adaptation
   needs a separate proposal. Git history preserves revisions; readers also
   need access to competing interpretations at the same time.
5. **Context can be part of the idea.**
   [Case 29](cases/c029-du-bois-double-consciousness.md) proposes a question
   about the racial context of double-consciousness. Including some context
   in a short version does not establish how much readers need.
6. **Describing a method may leave out how to use it.** The methodological and narrative
   cases describe ways of comparing, questioning, or examining examples.
   Whether readers can carry out those activities needs testing.
7. **An objection needs a clear target.** The
   [induction argument](../arguments/a001-induction.md) groups two premises.
   Its [open question](../questions/q002-circularity.md) concerns what the
   inference establishes. The links record this distinction; they do not
   verify the argument.

## Expected context needs

Each [study case](CORPUS.md) records the main context expected to be needed
for its reader question, using the [protocol's categories](PROTOCOL.md#expected-context-needs).
These expectations concern the particular question, not understanding the
whole passage. When a revision puts a necessary distinction into the short
version, the expected context need may change as well.

The expectations are editorial judgments, not reader results or estimates of
how much philosophy can be shortened. Several kinds of context may be needed
together; only reader testing can establish which versions are adequate.

## Length and reader effort

The [structural report](STRUCTURAL-CHECK.md) gives current word counts for the
short versions and for the short versions with context and limits. It is
regenerated from the files, so it remains separate from these observations.

Those counts exclude source passages, concept pages, and other linked
material. They do not measure total reading effort or show that meaning has
been preserved. Word counts do not show the effort of reconstructing missing context.

## What remains untested

The study has not measured reader understanding, reviewer agreement, the
usefulness of sense distinctions, sensitivity to translation, or the effort
needed to write and review entries. Fuller argument, historical, and narrative
materials still need preparation for many cases.

The sample favors familiar, accessible passages and has substantial gaps,
described in the [sampling limits](CORPUS.md#sampling-limits). Prior familiarity
may conceal missing explanations. Later samples should include unfamiliar
passages and alternative translations.

## Next comparison

Obtain independent source review, prepare the missing context, and record the
exact study materials in Git. Then compare the reading versions defined in
the [reader study protocol](PROTOCOL.md), measuring accuracy and effort
separately. The aim is the shortest tested version that preserves the
distinctions needed for a specified task. This pilot has not yet reached that
result.
