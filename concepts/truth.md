# Truth

These passages examine conviction answerable to reality and the distinction between ordinary and ultimate truth.

## Conviction secured against rational rejection ([Maimonides](../people/maimonides.md))

A conviction that corresponds to reality and admits no reasonable alternative, in the argument about divine unity.

Related proposals:

- [Belief requires understanding](../proposals/p079-maimonides-belief.md)

## Ordinary and ultimate truth ([Nāgārjuna](../people/nagarjuna.md))

Two registers of teaching; ultimate understanding requires ordinary conventions.

Related proposals:

- [Ordinary conventions and ultimate understanding](../proposals/p083-nagarjuna-two-truths.md)

## Correctness beyond winning assent ([Zhuangzi](../people/zhuangzi.md))

What makes an assertion correct, questioned independently of victory or agreement.

Related proposals:

- [Winning a dispute does not settle correctness](../proposals/p094-zhuangzi-dispute.md)
