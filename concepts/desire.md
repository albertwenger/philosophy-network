# Desire

Desire can name our striving to act, an attachment we cultivate, or a demand whose limits we can reconsider.

## A person’s striving toward activity ([Spinoza](../people/spinoza.md))

A person’s actual striving as directed toward a particular activity. It includes impulses and appetites that may conflict with one another.

Related proposals:

- [Desire as directed striving](../proposals/p104-spinoza-desire-and-striving.md)

## Wanting less through gratitude ([Kimmerer](../people/kimmerer.md))

The appetite for more can subside when existing gifts are experienced as enough.

Related proposals:

- [Gratitude can change how much we take](../proposals/p112-kimmerer-gratitude-and-enough.md)

## An attachment acquired as an end in itself ([Mill](../people/mill.md))

Desiring something first valued as a means until it becomes part of one’s happiness, as virtue can.

Related proposals:

- [An acquired end can become part of happiness](../proposals/p127-mill-virtue-as-part-of-happiness.md)

## Wants directed toward what is up to us ([Epictetus](../people/epictetus.md))

Desire disciplined so that another person’s control over external outcomes does not become control over one’s own freedom.

Related proposals:

- [Desire can make us dependent on others](../proposals/p121-epictetus-desire-and-dependence.md)
