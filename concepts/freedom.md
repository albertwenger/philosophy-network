# Freedom

Freedom can concern limits on coercion, independence in judgment and desire, or the joint activity of sense and reason.

## Freedom from coercion ([Mill](../people/mill.md))

Freedom from coercion when one’s conduct does not harm others. [Mill](../people/mill.md) restricts the principle to mature people in societies he considers civilized.

Related proposals:

- [The harm principle and its historical scope](../proposals/p014-mill-harm.md)

- [Circumstances can turn expression into instigation](../proposals/p120-mill-expression-and-instigation.md)

## Freedom in what is up to us ([Epictetus](../people/epictetus.md))

Freedom in one’s own judgments and choices, excluding external possessions and outcomes. This is a strict division, not a scale of partial influence.

Related proposals:

- [What is up to us](../proposals/p018-epictetus-control.md)

- [Desire can make us dependent on others](../proposals/p121-epictetus-desire-and-dependence.md)

## Freedom through the joint activity of sense and reason ([Schiller](../people/schiller.md))

The mind’s capacity to act when neither sensation nor rational form exclusively constrains it.

Related proposals:

- [An active middle between sense and reason](../proposals/p102-schiller-aesthetic-middle.md)
