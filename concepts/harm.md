# Harm

Harm can mean injury that bears on coercion, violence excused as development, or damage to shared surroundings.

## Injury to others ([Mill](../people/mill.md))

Injury to other people. Within the principle’s restricted historical scope, [Mill](../people/mill.md) allows coercion only to prevent such harm; it does not justify every coercive response.

Related proposals:

- [The harm principle and its historical scope](../proposals/p014-mill-harm.md)

- [Circumstances can turn expression into instigation](../proposals/p120-mill-expression-and-instigation.md)

## Violence concealed by a civilizing justification ([Dussel](../people/dussel.md))

Suffering imposed on colonized peoples and others, then excused as the cost of development.

Related proposals:

- [Progress can conceal its victims](../proposals/p115-dussel-costs-of-civilizing.md)

## The loss of shared natural beauty ([Morris](../people/morris.md))

Damage to surroundings whose beauty is held in common and contributes to other people’s lives.

Related proposals:

- [Damaging shared beauty can injure others](../proposals/p124-morris-protecting-common-beauty.md)
