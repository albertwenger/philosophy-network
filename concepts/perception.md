# Perception

These passages distinguish sensory experience, the combination of sensations, and the understanding of what is perceived.

## Sensing through bodily organs ([Plato](../people/plato.md))

Seeing, hearing, and other sensations received through bodily organs, distinguished from reflection on their relations.

Related proposals:

- [Knowledge requires reflection on sensation](../proposals/p065-plato-sensation-reflection.md)

## Recognizing a body through understanding ([Descartes](../people/descartes.md))

Apprehending what a body is through mental judgment, beyond its current appearance.

Related proposals:

- [The wax through its alterations](../proposals/p069-descartes-wax.md)

## Impressions and ideas ([Hume](../people/hume.md))

Mental experiences distinguished by their force: lively impressions and fainter ideas.

Related proposals:

- [Ideas and their experiential materials](../proposals/p072-hume-impressions-ideas.md)

## Direct experience with a limited view ([Dōgen](../people/dogen.md))

Embodied experience that discloses one side while another remains unseen.

Related proposals:

- [Seeing with the whole body and mind](../proposals/p087-dogen-embodied-seeing.md)

## The combination of sensory information ([Avicenna](../people/avicenna.md))

An internal capacity that brings different senses’ information together.

Related proposals:

- [Combining what different senses disclose](../proposals/p091-avicenna-common-sense.md)
